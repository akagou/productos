# Synthesis: October 2026 customer interviews

## Method and participants
No research plan exists. 4 interviews by Priya Raman and Lena Cho, notes typed during the call. Source: product/research/raw/interviews-2026-10.md (gitignored). Four firms is a small, unscreened sample: all current customers, none who churned. Quotes are as typed in the notes.

| ID | Segment | Date |
|---|---|---|

## Findings
| ID | Finding | Sources | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | Urgent tenant messages (burst pipe, lockout, gas, no heat) are missed or seen hours late, because they look like every other message or never reach the on-call person. | P1, P2, P3, P4 | Observed |
| F2 | Interviewees did not ask for AI replies unprompted. Where they discussed them, they doubted the need (P1) or set conditions: sourced facts (P2), no promised dates (P3). | P1, P2, P3 | Observed |
| F3 | A wrong statement to a tenant has cost real money (about $1,900 in lost rent). | P2 | Told |
| F4 | One owner spends evenings on repeat questions (23 in a week about parking, trash day, rent portal) and wants them answered without staff. | P3 | Told |
| F5 | A 450-unit firm is evaluating a competitor with AI replies before its January 2027 renewal. | P4 | Told |
| F6 | Assigning a maintenance request to a vendor takes 6 steps and a phone call. | P4 | Told |
| F7 | Staff stay in the inbox and rarely touch new features, so a feature outside the inbox is unlikely to be used. | P4 | Told |

## Observations by theme
### Urgent messages get lost
- P1: a burst-pipe text at 7:12 was seen at 10:20; about $2,800 damage to the unit below (paraphrase)
- P1: "it looked like every other text." (checked the inbox at 7:30 and scrolled past it)
- P1: "I don't need help writing. I need to know what's on fire."
- P2: texts to the main number do not reach the on-call phone (paraphrase)
- P3: missed one lockout text this year, saw it the next morning (paraphrase)
- P4: twice this quarter an urgent text sat in the shared inbox overnight (paraphrase)

### AI replies: conditional interest
- P2: "if it gets the lease details right."
- P2: wants any AI reply to show where it got the facts (paraphrase)
- P3: worried an AI would promise a repair date she cannot keep (paraphrase)
- P4: saw a competitor demo with AI replies (paraphrase)

### Repeat questions
- P3: "If those answered themselves, I'd get my evenings back."

### Workflow and adoption
- P4: "They rarely touch new features. They live in the inbox."
- P4: "anything that saves my coordinators an hour a day."
- P2: tenants say replies feel robotic (paraphrase)

## Contradictions
- P1 says writing is not the problem; P2 and P3 see value in AI help if constrained. P2's need is reply quality, P3's is volume.
- P3's want (repeat questions answering themselves) is not the same as drafts that staff still review.

## Not answered
- How often urgent messages are missed across all 62 firms (see ticket analysis).
- Whether firms would pay for AI drafts or for urgent-message alerts.
- Why firms that left, left. No churned customer was interviewed.
- Whether the 29% of paid seats that are not weekly active are blocked by anything these interviews touch.

## Proposed opportunities
- Staff cannot tell which tenant message is an emergency, so it waits hours (Guess, from F1)
- Urgent texts do not reach whoever is on call (Guess, from F1)
- Repeat questions consume staff time that could go to real problems (Guess, from F4)
- Replying accurately is hard when facts live in leases and spreadsheets (Guess, from F2, F3)
- Assigning a repair to a vendor takes too many steps (Guess, from F6)
