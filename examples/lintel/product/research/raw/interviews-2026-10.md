# Interview notes, October 2026 (fictional)

Interviews by Priya Raman and Lena Cho. Notes typed during the call. Names changed.

## Dana, office manager, 120 units, 4 staff (2026-10-06)

- Last Tuesday she had 37 messages in the inbox before 10am.
- A tenant texted about a burst pipe at 7:12. Dana saw it at 10:20. Water damage to the unit below: about $2,800.
- "I don't need help writing. I need to know what's on fire."
- Uses Lintel plus a whiteboard of open repairs. Moves urgent items to the whiteboard by hand.
- Checked the inbox on her phone at 7:30 but scrolled past it: "it looked like every other text."

## Marcus, operations lead, 300 units, 9 staff (2026-10-08)

- His team uses Lintel templates and copies text into replies. Tenants say replies feel robotic.
- Likes the idea of AI drafts "if it gets the lease details right."
- Story: last spring a temp told a tenant the wrong move-out date. The firm lost a month of rent, about $1,900.
- Wants any AI reply to show where it got the facts.
- Emergencies: has an on-call phone, but texts to the main number do not reach it.

## Aisha, owner, 60 units, alone plus one part-time assistant (2026-10-13)

- Answers tenants from her phone at night.
- Counted last week: 23 questions about parking, trash day or the rent portal.
- "If those answered themselves, I'd get my evenings back."
- Worried an AI would promise a repair date she cannot keep.
- Has missed one lockout text this year, saw it the next morning.

## Ken, director, 450 units, 14 staff (2026-10-20)

- Saw a Doorbell PM demo with AI replies. Is evaluating a switch before his renewal in January 2027.
- Asked what his team would use: "They rarely touch new features. They live in the inbox."
- Biggest daily friction: assigning a maintenance request to a vendor takes 6 steps and a phone call.
- Would pay more for "anything that saves my coordinators an hour a day."
- Emergencies go to an on-call rotation by phone. Twice this quarter an urgent text sat in the shared inbox overnight.
